AML & Fraud Detection Guidelines

Anti-Money Laundering (AML) is a set of procedures, laws, and regulations designed to prevent criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate income.

Financial institutions must continuously monitor customer transactions and identify suspicious activities.

Common money laundering typologies include:

1. Structuring
Structuring involves breaking a large amount of cash into several smaller deposits below the reporting threshold to avoid regulatory detection.

2. Layering
Layering refers to transferring money through multiple accounts, banks, or countries to hide its original source.

3. Integration
Integration occurs when illicit funds are reintroduced into the legitimate financial system through investments or business transactions.

Indicators of suspicious transactions include:

• Frequent cash deposits below reporting limits.
• Rapid movement of funds between multiple accounts.
• Round-trip transactions.
• Transactions involving shell companies.
• High-risk foreign jurisdictions.
• Unusual transaction velocity.

Sanctions Screening

Banks must screen customers and beneficiaries against international sanctions lists including:

- OFAC Sanctions List
- United Nations Sanctions List
- European Union Sanctions List

Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)

PEPs are individuals who hold prominent public positions and therefore require Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD).

Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR)

When suspicious activity is detected, compliance officers prepare a Suspicious Activity Report containing:

- Customer Information
- Account Details
- Transaction Summary
- Investigation Notes
- AML Typology
- Risk Score
- Recommended Actions

Regulatory filing should generally occur within the prescribed reporting timeline after suspicious activity has been identified.

Enhanced Due Diligence should be performed for high-risk customers and continuous monitoring should be maintained until the investigation is closed.
